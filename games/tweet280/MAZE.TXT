"MAZE for B8Script, in a tweet. A new 19 by 9 maze every game, made by a random";
"walk, so every maze is as likely as any other. You are @, in the top right cell;";
"get out through the gap at the bottom left. When you get out it says how many";
"keys you pressed. Making the maze takes a while on an 8-bit machine: about 10";
"seconds on a 4 MHz Z80 and 30 at 1 MHz, more for some mazes.";
"Keys: i up, j left, k down, l right. Needs a console with the screen escapes.";

"Variables";
"  r  random state   g  the maze: a heap block of 19 rows of 41 bytes, and a";
"     quote after it, so that g@$ prints it";
"  i  while the maze is made, the address of the walk's cell; then your place in";
"     the block, row * 41 + column";
"  w t  the addresses of the wall the walk crosses and the cell it steps to";
"  The cells left to visit, and then the count of keys pressed, are on the stack.";

"Print the title while waiting for a key, counting; the count is the seed.";
"ijkl. Key, wait"$0 1(1+1?0=)r!

"The maze, all walls: note the heap top in g, then 19 rows of 39 # (35) and a";
"  cell of 2573, which is CR (13) and LF (10) as two bytes. Then a quote (34).";
"  Each loop's count is its own entry flag (19:( and 39:( ).";
0_g!19:(39:(35 1_.1-:);2573 2_!1-:);34 1_.

"D ( k -- step ): the low two bits of k pick a character from the string Q'Oy,";
"  whose codes less 80 are +1, -41, -1 and +41: right, up, left and down.";
[D3&"Q'Oy"+,80-]

"The exit: a space at place 697 (row 17, column 0). The walk starts in the top";
"  right cell, place 78 (row 1, column 37), so that is a space too, and i is its";
"  address. 170 cells are left to visit.";
32:g@697+.g@78+:i!.170

"The walk (Aldous-Broder). Cells are at odd rows and columns. Each pass:";
"  1. Step the random state, r * 5 + 1, which goes through all 65536 values, and";
"     take bits 12 and 13 for D: a half step h. w is i + h, the wall between,";
"     and t is w + h, the next cell.";
"  2. The cell's byte, c, if t is in rows 1 to 17 (41 < t - g < 735); else 0. A";
"     step off the left or right edge lands on an LF or a CR.";
"  3. If c is over 31 (# or a space), the walk moves to t.";
"  4. If c is # (35), the cell is new: open the wall and the cell with spaces,";
"     and add the flag, -1, to the cells left.";
"  5. Go on until no cells are left. The 0 left is the key count.";
"  The maze is a tree, as the walk opens a wall only into a cell it has never";
"  been in: every cell can be reached, by just one way.";
:(r@5*1+:r!4096/;D:i@+:w!+:t!
  g@-:41>%735<&t@,&
  :31>(t@i!0)
  35=:(32:w@.t@.0)+
:)

"Clear the screen, print the maze, and put you at place 78.";
"/{"$g@$78i!

"The game is one loop, a key a pass. Each pass:";
"  1. Draw @ at your place, row i // 41 and column the remainder.";
"  2. Wait for a key, and D turns it into a step: l, i, j and k give right, up,";
"     left and down. Another key gives one of these too.";
"  3. The step is kept only if the cell it leads to is a space (code 32); else 0.";
"  4. Wipe the @ at the old place with a space, move, and add 1 to the count";
"     (a key that bumps a wall counts as a key).";
"  5. The loop flag is the new place less 697: go on until you are out.";
1(i@41/"/~@"$
  0?D
  :i@+g@+,32=&
  i@41/"/~ "$
  i@+:i!697-
  %1+%
)

"The end: row 20, column 0: the count of keys.";
20 0"/~Out in "$#" keys"$
