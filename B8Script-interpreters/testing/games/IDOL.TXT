"IDOL for B8Script. A space trading game on an 80 x 24 ANSI screen.";
"You start in debt with a small ship, the Courier. Trade between the 64 star";
"systems of a generated galaxy, smuggle when it pays, fight or flee, take";
"contracts, upgrade your ship, and track down the six parts of an ancient idol,";
"which can only be bartered for. Win by holding all six with the loan repaid;";
"the score is the number of days it took. The game ends at day 999.";
"The rules and numbers come from docs/design/trading-game.md, the design doc;";
"where a note below names a number (the tank, the normal stock N, and so on) it";
"is the doc's.";
"Keys when docked: c chart, m market, y shipyard, k take a contract, l pay the";
"loan, f refuel, r repair, t trade for an idol part, v show the save code,";
"? help, q quit. In the chart, market and shipyard: w and s move, Return jumps";
"(chart), b buys and x sells (then type a quantity; Return alone is the most).";

"How to read the notes. ( a b -- c ) is a stack effect: what a word takes and leaves,";
"the top of the stack on the right. [X ... ] defines word X; calling X runs its body.";
"Every comment is a string that is dropped at once (the semicolon), so it does nothing.";
"This file is the documentation: it is too big to load on a small machine. IDOL.B8S is";
"the same program with the comments and spaces stripped (b8strip.py), and that is the";
"one to run. No slash is written in any comment, so a division is spelt out.";

"The plan in short:";
"  1. B8Script has 26 words and 26 variables, too few for a program this size. So the";
"     game's state lives in one block of cells (G gives a cell's address by number),";
"     the galaxy in another, and most routines are numbered: [ body] n:+z@+! puts the";
"     body's address in slot n of a table, and n Z calls it. Letters are kept for the";
"     small words that run most.";
"  2. The galaxy is generated from the seed when a game starts (or loads): positions,";
"     types, safety, the six idol parts. Names, prices, offers and rumours are worked";
"     out when needed from the seed, the system and the day, so nothing about them is";
"     stored or saved. Only the stock levels are kept for every system.";
"  3. The screen is drawn once, then only what changes is redrawn: the status line (S),";
"     the side panel, the main area (chart, market, shipyard, duel or help) and the";
"     three message lines, which are kept in a buffer so they can scroll.";
"  4. The main loop reads a key and runs it. A jump passes a day and may bring an";
"     encounter (pirates, police, debt collectors) settled by a short duel.";

"Variables";
"  g  the state block: 50 cells, numbered (see below); n G is the address of cell n";
"  h  the galaxy block (see below)   m  the message buffer   z  the routine table";
"  o  where printed characters go: 0 the screen, else the next byte of a message";
"  r  random state   v  the view: 0 chart, 1 market, 2 shipyard, 3 help";
"  u  the system chosen on the chart (-1 none)   w  the row chosen in the market";
"     or the shipyard   q  0 while playing; why the game ended (see the main loop)";
"  a b c d e f i j k l n p s x y  scratch; each routine's note says which it uses";

"The state block g: cell n is at g+2n, and n G gives that address. Cells 0 to 33 are";
"the player's state, saved in the save code in this order; the rest are working cells.";
"   0 seed      1 day        2 cash high  3 cash low   4 debt        5 position";
"   6-13 cargo, tonnes of goods 0 to 7    14 ship class (0 Courier 1 Freighter";
"   2 Raider 3 Clipper)  15 laser level  16 shield level  17 missiles  18 cargo pods";
"  19 tank upgrades  20 hull  21 fuel  22 escape pod (0 or 1)  23 hidden hold (0 or 1)";
"  24 wanted days  25 idol parts held (a bit each)  26 idol parts known (a bit each)";
"  27 contract kind (0 none, 1 carry, 2 bounty)  28 its destination  29 its good";
"  30 its tonnes  31 its deadline day  32 its pay  33 passengers (1) or sealed cargo (0)";
"  34-41 the duel: enemy hull, its full hull, laser, shield, missiles, the range";
"        (0 far, 1 close), the enemy's kind (0 pirate 1 police 2 collector), its level";
"  42-48 an offer being looked at, in the order of 27 to 33";
"Money is two cells, high and low, meaning high*10000+low, with low always 0 to 9999,";
"as the doc's economy part says: cash goes far past 32767, the largest cell. Every";
"single amount added or taken stays below 32767, so one cell holds it.";

"The galaxy block h, 1032 bytes, for system i (0 to 63; 0 is home):";
"  h+i       x on the chart, 0 to 55     h+64+i   y on the chart, 0 to 15";
"  h+128+i   kind byte: type (0 Agricultural 1 Mining 2 Industrial 3 High-tech) in";
"            bits 0-1, safety (0 policed 1 frontier 2 lawless) in bits 2-3, and";
"            visited in bit 4 (value 16)";
"  h+192+8i+k  the stock of good k, 0 to 60 tonnes, a byte";
"  h+704+2i  the day that system's stock was last brought up to date, a cell";
"  h+832+p   the system where idol part p (0 to 5) lies";
"  h+840+2i  the chart's key for system i, a cell (see 24)";
"  h+968+i   the character the chart shows for system i, or 0 if none (see 24)";

"The message buffer m: three slots of 80 bytes, each a message of at most 78";
"characters and a quote after it, so $ can print a slot. Byte m+240 says which slot";
"holds the newest message; the other two are the older ones. A new message goes in";
"the oldest slot, so nothing is copied.";

"The screen (rows and columns from 1):";
"  row 1      the status line";
"  row 2      a border: + then 56 dashes, + then 21 dashes, +";
"  rows 3-18  the main area, columns 2 to 57, and the side panel, columns 59 to 79,";
"             between bars in columns 1, 58 and 80";
"  row 19     a border; rows 20-22 the messages; row 23 the command line;";
"  row 24     the bottom border, which stops at column 79: writing the last column";
"             of the last row would scroll the screen on some terminals";
"  The chart puts system i at row y+3, column x+2.";

"Allocate the blocks. The heap top is the address of each new block.";
100_g! 1032_h! 241_m! 128_z! 0o! 0m@240+.

"Words";

"R: ( n -- 0..n-1 )  a random number below n. r is a linear congruential generator:";
"   r becomes r*25173+13849, wrapping at 65536 (the multiplier and the step make the";
"   generator go through all 65536 states). Its low bits are poor, so dividing by 256";
"   and masking with 255 takes the upper byte (0 to 255); the swap brings n up and";
"   the remainder of the division is the number. For a state above 32767 the cell";
"   reads as negative and the division rounds toward zero, which gives one more than";
"   the high byte (255 turning into 0) unless the low byte is 0: still 0 to 255.";
[R r@25173*13849+:r! 256/;255& %/%;]

"Y: ( row column -- )  move the cursor, counted from 1: ESC [ row ; column H. 27' and";
"   91' print ESC and the bracket, the swap brings the row up to print first, 59' is";
"   the semicolon, # prints the column and 72' is the H. This and the three strings";
"   that start 27' (clear the screen, clear a line, clear to its end) are all the";
"   screen control there is: ESC is printed with 27' so no string needs an escape.";
[Y27'91'%#59'#72']

"Z: ( n -- )  call routine n. n doubled plus z is its slot; @ fetches the address,";
"   { puts it on the return stack and ] jumps to it, as if Z had been called from";
"   there. The routine's own ] then returns to whoever called Z.";
[Z:+z@+@{]

"G: ( n -- address )  the address of state cell n: g+2n.";
[G:+g@+]

"E: ( c -- )  print the character c: to the screen when o is 0, otherwise into the";
"   message being written, at o, moving o on. The guard copies the flag, so one copy";
"   opens the block (the swap brings c up for ' to print) and the other leaves the";
"   word with the break.";
[E o@0=:(%'0)\ o@. o@1+o!]

"T: ( address -- )  print a string through E, up to its closing quote. Each pass";
"   fetches the next character; a quote (34) breaks out, leaving the address and the";
"   quote, which the two drops take away.";
[T 1(:,:34=\ E 1+ 1);;]

"N: ( n -- )  print n (0 to 32767) through E. The digits are worked out by dividing";
"   by 10, the last digit first, so they are pushed on the stack above a -1 marker:";
"   each pass leaves the digit character (remainder plus 48) under the quotient, and";
"   stops when the quotient is 0, which is dropped. Then the second loop prints the";
"   top digit, the first one, until the marker comes up, and drops it.";
[N -1% 1(10/ 48+% :0=0=); 1(E :0<0=);]

"A: ( n w -- )  print n right-aligned in w columns, through E. The digits of n are";
"   1 plus one for each of 9, 99, 999 and 9999 that n is above; a true comparison is";
"   -1, so w-1 plus the four flags is the number of spaces. n waits on the return";
"   stack while the flags are made (each copy of n is swapped up for its test).";
[A 1-%:{:9>%:99>%:999>%9999>++++ 1(:1<\32E1- 1); }N]

"M: ( -- )  begin a new message: the slot after the newest (3 wraps to 0) becomes";
"   the newest, and o points at its start, so that E, T and N write into it.";
[M m@240+:,1+:3=(;0 0)%. m@240+,80*m@+o!]

"F: ( -- )  finish the message: a quote ends it, printing goes back to the screen,";
"   and the three slots are shown on rows 20 to 22, the oldest at the top. Row 19+k";
"   shows slot (newest+k) mod 3 for k from 1 to 3, so the newest is on row 22. Each";
"   is printed, the rest of its line cleared, and the bar at column 80 put back; the";
"   row waits under k on the stack, swapped up when it is needed.";
[F 34o@. 0o! 1 1(:19+:2Y %:m@240+,+3/%; 80*m@+$ 27'"[K"$ %80Y124' 1+:4<);]

"C: ( n -- )  add n to the cash (n may be negative, as small as -32767). One division";
"   by 10000 splits n into a high part and a low part, both with n's sign; they are";
"   added to the two cells. The low cell is then -9999 to 19998, and one carry or one";
"   borrow puts it back into 0 to 9999.";
[C 10000/ 3G@+3G! 2G@+2G!
   3G@9999>(3G@10000- 3G! 2G@1+2G! 0) 3G@0<(3G@10000+3G! 2G@1- 2G! 0)]

"W: ( -- n )  the wallet: the cash, or 30000 if there is more. Every price is below";
"   30000, so this is all a purchase needs to compare with. A high part above 2 means";
"   30000 or more; otherwise high*10000+low is at most 29999 and fits a cell.";
[W 2G@2>:(30000%0)\ 2G@10000*3G@+]

"D: ( i j -- q )  four times the squared distance between systems i and j, with x";
"   counted at half (a screen cell is about twice as tall as it is wide): dx*dx +";
"   4*dy*dy, at most 3025+900. j waits on the return stack, then i above it, while";
"   the x difference is squared; then each comes back for the y difference.";
[D:{h@+,%:{h@+,-:*}h@+64+,}h@+64+,-:*:+:++]

"J: ( q -- c )  the distance as the fuel a jump costs: the real distance, the square";
"   root of q divided by 2, rounded up. That is the smallest c with 4c*c >= q. Rather";
"   than square c each time, the loop takes 8c+4 (the step from 4c*c to 4(c+1)(c+1))";
"   off what is left of q, while anything is left; the count of passes is c. Inside";
"   a pass the stack is c t: the swap, the copy and the rotation make c t 8c+4 into";
"   c and t minus 8c+4, then c is counted up. A jump in range takes at most 14 passes.";
[J 0% 1(:0>0=\ %:8*4+{%}%%- %1+% 1);]

"H: ( n -- )  start the random state from the seed and n: r becomes (seed+n) times";
"   the multiplier plus the step, then two draws mix it further. Names, prices,";
"   offers, rumours and idol prices each call H with their own n, so they come out";
"   the same every time they are worked out, and in a loaded game too.";
[H 0G@+ 25173*13849+ r! 1R; 1R;]

"K: ( i -- byte )  the kind byte of system i: its type, safety and visited bit.";
[K h@+128+,]

"L: ( i -- )  print the name of system i through E. The name is 2 or 3 syllables";
"   taken from a table of 32 two-letter syllables (64 bytes), chosen by H from the";
"   seed and 1000+i, so it is the same every time; i = 64 names the idol. The first";
"   letter is made a capital by masking it with 223, which clears bit 5. The random";
"   state is kept on the return stack and put back at the end, so that printing a";
"   name never upsets the numbers a duel is drawing. Inside the loop the stack is";
"   mask count: the mask waits on the return stack while a syllable s (32R) is";
"   found at the table plus 2s; its first letter is masked and printed, its second";
"   printed, and the mask becomes 255 for the rest of the name.";
[L r@{ 1000+H 223 2R2+ 1(%{32R:+"laveraxedisoticebumazerikoangeloquvaneisortausbearonmidaensukael"+:,}&E1+,E 255%1-:);; }r!]

"X: ( k w address -- )  print entry k of a table of words, each w characters wide and";
"   padded with spaces, through E. The address is parked while k*w is made, then";
"   the characters are printed up to the first space.";
[X{*}+ 1(:,:32=\E1+1);;]

"B: ( f c -- v )  stat f of ship class c, from a table of six letters a class, each";
"   64 plus the value: f is 0 cargo (in tens of tonnes), 1 tank, 2 hull (in tens),";
"   3 laser maximum, 4 shield maximum, 5 price (in thousands). The rows are the doc's";
"   class table: Courier BGCBBE (20 7 30 2 2 5000), Freighter EHEBCI, Raider CJFDDL,";
"   Clipper GJHDD^ (^ is 64+30).";
[B 6*+"BGCBBEEHEBCICJFDDLGJHDD^"+,64-]

"V: ( -- n )  tonnes in the hold: the cargo of the eight goods, plus a carry";
"   contract's tonnes (sealed cargo and passengers both take hold space). The start";
"   value is the contract's tonnes masked by the flag kind = 1. Then for cells 6 to";
"   13 the sum, under the cell number, is rotated up and added.";
[V 27G@1=30G@& 6 1(:G@{%}%+%1+:14<);]

"Routines. Each is [ body] then its number n and :+z@+!, which stores the body's";
"address in slot n of z. The note before each gives n, the stack effect, and the";
"scratch variables it uses.";

"0: ( n c -- )  print character c n times, straight to the screen.";
[ %1(%:'%1-:);;] 0:+z@+!

"1: ( -- )  print the cash through E: the high part and then the low part padded";
"   to four digits with zeros, or just the low part when the high part is 0.";
[ 2G@:0=:(%;3G@N0)\ N 3G@:1000<(48E0):100<(48E0):10<(48E0)N] 1:+z@+!

"2: ( -- )  clear the main area: 56 spaces on each of rows 18 down to 3.";
[ 16 1(:2+ 2Y "                                                        "$ 1-:);] 2:+z@+!

"3: ( -- )  draw the frame on a cleared screen: the borders on rows 2, 19 and 24 and";
"   the bars down the sides (and between the main area and the side panel).";
[ 27'"[2J"$ 27'"[H"$
   2 1Y 43' 56 45 0Z 43' 21 45 0Z 43'  19 1Y 43' 56 45 0Z 43' 21 45 0Z 43'
   16 1(:2+:1Y 124' :58Y 124' 80Y 124' 1-:);
   20 1(:1Y 124' :80Y 124' 1+:24<);
   24 1Y 43' 78 45 0Z] 3:+z@+!

"4 to 8: ( k -- )  print the name of a type, a good, a safety level, a ship class or";
"   an idol part through E, from tables of fixed-width words (X).";
[ 9"Agri     Mining   Industry Hi-tech  "X] 4:+z@+!
[ 10"Food      Textiles  Ore       Machinery Medicine  Luxuries  Narcotics Weapons   "X] 5:+z@+!
[ 9"Policed  Frontier Lawless  "X] 6:+z@+!
[ 10"Courier   Freighter Raider    Clipper   "X] 7:+z@+!
[ 6"Head  Eye   Heart LHand RHand Base  "X] 8:+z@+!

"9: ( -- n )  the hold: the class's cargo plus 10 tonnes for each cargo pod.";
"10: ( -- n )  the tank: the class's tank plus 2 for each tank upgrade.";
"11: ( -- n )  the class's full hull.";
[ 0 14G@B10* 18G@10*+] 9:+z@+!
[ 1 14G@B 19G@:++] 10:+z@+!
[ 2 14G@B10*] 11:+z@+!

"12: ( -- )  draw the side panel. Uses i j k. Rows 3 to 18 are cleared first.";
"   Row 3: the class and its laser, shield and missiles. Row 4: tonnes held of the";
"   hold. Rows 5 on: each good held, its name at column 61 and its tonnes right-";
"   aligned to column 77. Row 15: the idol parts held and, when it runs, the wanted";
"   count. In the chart view rows 16 to 18 show the chosen system (15).";
[ 16 1(:2+ 59Y "                     "$ 1-:);
   3 60Y 14G@7Z " L"$ 15G@# " S"$ 16G@# " M"$ 17G@#
   4 60Y "Cargo "$ V# 47' 9Z#
   5j! 0i! 1( i@6+G@( j@61Y i@5Z j@74Y i@6+G@4A j@1+j! 0) i@1+:i!8<)
   15 60Y "Idol "$ 23Z# " of 6"$ 24G@( "  Wanted "$ 24G@# 0)
   v@0=(15Z 0)] 12:+z@+!

"23: ( -- n )  how many idol parts are held: the bits of cell 25 counted. Uses k.";
"   Each pass adds the low bit to k and halves the value, six times.";
[ 0k! 25G@6 1(%:1&k@+k!2/;%1-:);; k@] 23:+z@+!

"S: ( -- )  draw the status line, row 1: where you are, its type and safety, the";
"   day, cash, debt, fuel of tank, and the hull as a percentage of the class's full";
"   hull. The row is cleared first, and each field goes to its own column.";
[S 1 1Y 27'"[2K"$ 1 2Y 5G@L 1 9Y 5G@K3& 4Z 1 18Y 5G@K4/;3& 6Z
   1 27Y "Day "$ 1G@# 1 36Y "Cr "$ 1Z 1 50Y "Debt "$ 4G@#
   1 61Y "Fuel "$ 21G@# 47' 10Z# 1 72Y "Hull "$ 20G@100* 11Z/;# 37']

"13: ( -- )  draw the chart from nothing: clear the main area, mark every system as";
"   not shown (0 in its byte at h+968), and let 24 draw them all. Uses i, and 24's.";
[ 2Z 0i! 1( 0i@h@+968+. i@1+:i! 64<) 24Z] 13:+z@+!

"24: ( -- )  bring the chart up to date. Uses i l p x y, and 14's a b c d j k.";
"   p is where you are and l the range: a system is in range when D from p is at";
"   most 4*fuel*fuel. For each system i the loop first works out its key for the";
"   chart's choices (14) and keeps it at h+840+2i: 64*q + i - 32768 for q = D, or";
"   32767 for a system out of range or your own. In range q is at most 784, so";
"   64*q + i is below 50240, and subtracting 32768 makes it fit a signed cell with";
"   the order kept: nearer first, and the lower number first when two are as far.";
"   (The if-else keeps its flag on the return stack for the else.) y is the";
"   in-range flag, -1 or 0.";
"   Then the system's character: @ where you are; a visited system shows its type";
"   letter A M I H, an unvisited one a star; capitals and stars in range, lower case";
"   and dots out of range. Exactly one of the blocks leaves it: a visited system's";
"   letter has 32 added (lower case) when out of range, an unvisited one is 46 (dot)";
"   less 4 when in range, which is 42 (star). Only a character that differs from";
"   the one shown (h+968+i) is printed, at the system's place, and remembered: after";
"   a jump most of the chart stays as it is, and is not sent to the terminal again.";
"   Last, the nearest system in range is chosen (14 from no choice) and shown in";
"   the panel (15).";
[ 5G@p! 21G@:*:+:+ l!
   0i! 1( p@i@D :l@>i@p@=|:{(;32767 0)}0=(64*i@+ -32768+ 0) :i@:+h@+840+! 32767< y!
      i@p@=(64 0) i@p@=0=( i@K x! x@16&(x@3&"AMIH"+,y@0=32&+0) x@16&0=(46 y@4*+0) 0)
      :i@h@+968+,=0=( :i@h@+968+. i@h@+64+,3+ i@h@+,2+Y :' 0);
   i@1+:i! 64<)
   -1u! 1 14Z 15Z] 24:+z@+!

"14: ( d -- )  step the chosen system u: d = 1 to the next farther in range, -1 to";
"   the next nearer, in the order of the keys the chart made (13). Uses a b c d j k.";
"   Multiplying the keys by d turns nearer into farther. a is the key of the chosen";
"   system (-32768, below every key, when there is none), b the best key found yet";
"   and c its system. Every system in range (key not 32767) whose key is above a";
"   and below b becomes the best. If one was found it is chosen; if not, u stays as";
"   it is, at the end of the list.";
[ d! u@0<(-32768 0) u@-1>(u@:+h@+840+@d@* 0) a! 32767b! -1c!
   0j! 1( j@:+h@+840+@k! k@32767<( k@d@*k! k@a@> k@b@<&(k@b! j@c! 0) 0) j@1+:j! 64<)
   c@-1>(c@u! 0)] 14:+z@+!

"15: ( -- )  show the chosen system in rows 16 to 18 of the side panel: its name and";
"   the fuel a jump there costs, its type, its safety. Rows 16 to 18 are cleared.";
[ 3 1(:15+ 59Y "                     "$ 1-:);
   u@0<(16 60Y "Nothing in range"$ 0)
   u@-1>(16 60Y u@L "  fuel "$ 5G@u@DJ# 17 60Y u@K3& 4Z 18 60Y u@K4/;3& 6Z 0)] 15:+z@+!

"16: ( -- )  put the terminal's cursor on what is chosen, so it shows: in the chart";
"   the chosen system (your own when there is none), in the market and the";
"   shipyard row w+4 at column 2, in the help view the command line.";
[ v@0=(u@0<(5G@ 0) u@-1>(u@ 0) :h@+64+,3+ %h@+,2+ Y 0)
   v@1= v@2=|(w@4+ 2Y 0) v@3=(23 2Y 0)] 16:+z@+!

"18: ( -- )  jump to the chosen system. With none: a message, and nothing more.";
"   The fuel is the distance J of D; a day passes, which adds 1% to the debt (the";
"   loan, never above 30000) and counts the wanted days down. Then you arrive (19)";
"   and the status line, the panel and the chart are brought up to date.";
[ u@0<:(M"No system is in range."T F0)\
   5G@u@DJ 21G@%- 21G! 1G@1+1G! 4G@:100/;+:30000>(;30000 0)4G! 24G@:0>+24G!
   u@5G! 19Z S 12Z 24Z] 18:+z@+!

"19: ( -- )  arrive at the system in cell 5: mark it visited (bit 4 of its kind";
"   byte) and say so.";
[ 5G@h@+128+:,16|%. M"Docked at "T 5G@L "."T F] 19:+z@+!

"20: ( -- )  generate the galaxy from the seed. Uses a b c e f i k p s x y.";
"   The random state starts from the seed, so the same seed always gives the same";
"   galaxy. Home, system 0, is at the centre (28, 8). Each new system i is grown";
"   from a random system p already placed (i R picks one of 0 to i-1): an offset";
"   a, b of -14 to 14 and -7 to 7, which must be in the starter's range (D at most";
"   196, 4 times 7*7), give the place x, y. It must be on the chart and at least 2";
"   cells from every other system, in x or in y. That is looked up in a grid e of";
"   58 x 18 bytes, the chart with a border of one cell, where every system placed";
"   has a 1: the 3 x 3 cells around the place must all be 0. The grid is taken from";
"   the heap and given back at the end. Every system is in the starter's range of";
"   one placed before it, so all can be reached with the starter's tank.";
"   Then each system gets a random type and a safety from its distance from home:";
"   policed up to 4, frontier up to 9, lawless beyond. D is compared with 4 times";
"   the squares of those (64 and 324) rather than taking the root. Last, the six idol";
"   parts go to six different systems other than home; a policed system is drawn";
"   again once, so they lie mostly on the frontier and beyond.";
[ 0G@r! 1044_e! 0i! 1( 0e@i@+. i@1+:i! 1044<)
   28h@. 8h@64+. 1e@551+.
   1i! 1( 1( i@R p! 29R14- a! 15R7- b! p@h@+,a@+x! p@h@+64+,b@+y!
             x@-1> x@56<& y@-1>& y@16<& a@:*b@:*:+:++ 197<& f!
             f@( y@1+58* x@+1+e@+s!
                 s@59-,s@58-,+s@57-,+s@1-,+s@,+s@1+,+s@57+,+s@58+,+s@59+,+ 0=f! 0)
